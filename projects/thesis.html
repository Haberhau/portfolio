Shein and Temu split
Spain's fashion market

Spain's fashion split · Master's thesis · EAE Business School Barcelona · 2026

13,486 scraped products and five analytical models point to a split in Spanish fashion: ultra-value disruptors on one side, established retailers on the other.

Price tells the story.

Median prices separate the five retailers into markedly different positions. The incumbent and disruptor distributions differ in the thesis's Mann–Whitney U test (p < 0.001).

Two datasets. Five models.

Scraped retail prices and Eurostat trade data bring a product-level and a market-level view to the same question. The analysis uses KMeans clustering, STL decomposition, Mann–Whitney U testing and Granger causality.

The thesis's fragmentation index peaks at 0.89 in 2021 and reaches 0.03 in 2025, which the analysis interprets as an established split in the market.

Written with Ahra Ko and Elias Saifildin for the MSc in Big Data & Analytics at EAE Business School Barcelona, 2026.

Build on clothes worth repairing.

The proposed response is Wardrobe-as-a-Service: a €35 monthly rotation of three Zara pieces. It builds on an asset ultra-value retailers struggle to match: clothing with enough value to repair.

A proposal, not a launched service. At the thesis's €25 standard repair cost, the repairable share is 64% for Zara, 98.6% for Mango and about 3% for Shein and Temu.
